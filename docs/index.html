How it started

An old Mi A3.
A speaker lying around.
One small idea.

Useful things do not stop being useful when we replace them.

This project started with an old phone and an old speaker that were no longer being used. Together, they became a dedicated music player for the workspace.

The phone already had a screen, storage, and more than enough computing power to play music. It just needed a new job.

Hushilume gives that hardware a simple place in everyday life: on a desk, on a shelf, or beside the chair where you read.

Less time in a drawer.
More life in your room.

Start with what you have

  1. Find a device.

A starting point

There is more life
in old technology.

Even a phone we no longer carry has useful computing power, a display, storage, and connections. Music is one way to put those parts back to work.

Hushilume is an open project for exploring what that hardware can become. Other uses may follow as the project grows.

For now, it plays music.

That is already a good reason to keep an old device in use. Something you had lying around can make the place where you spend your day feel more pleasant.

A few practical details

Before you find that old phone.

What can I install it on?

The project includes an Android APK for personal testing and source for iPhone and iPad. It began on a Mi A3. Other devices may need screen-shape adjustments.

Does it need the internet?

Imported audio files play offline. YouTube, Spotify, and SoundCloud players need an internet connection. Available playback depends on the provider and your account.

Is the music in the demo included?

No. The demo uses a personal media collection. Import your own audio, covers, and videos into the public app.

Can I change or build on it?

Yes. The code and original Hushilume artwork use the MIT license. Explore the source, adapt it for your device, or contribute an improvement.
